How a five-colour print is made

A screen print is built one colour at a time, and each colour is a separate screen, a separate pull and a separate day of drying. Viaduct, Late Summer took five weeks for forty prints, which works out at about fifty-five minutes of work for each one — and that is if nothing goes wrong.

Things go wrong. A screen that has not been washed properly leaves a ghost of the previous print; ink dries in the mesh if the studio is warm; paper picks up a fingerprint that only appears under the fourth colour. Roughly one print in six is destroyed, which is why the edition is forty and not fifty.

The registration marks are the part nobody sees. Every sheet is lined up against the same two pins, and if a sheet moves half a millimetre between the third and fourth colour, the print goes in the bin — because you will see it, even if you cannot say what is wrong.

We open the workshop on the first Saturday of every month. Come and pull one yourself; it takes about an hour and you keep the result.
