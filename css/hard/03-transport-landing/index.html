What Loop does

Three things, properly, instead of thirty things badly.

  • Live departures

    Times from the operator's own feed, updated every ten seconds, with the vehicle's position when the operator publishes it. When a service is cancelled Loop says so instead of quietly removing it.

    See a live stop

  • Tickets that work offline

    Buy once, and the ticket lives on the phone. It scans in a tunnel, on a dead battery saver, and on a phone with no signal at all.

    How tickets work

  • Step-free routes

    Plan a journey that avoids stairs, steep gradients and broken lifts, using live lift status from the operators. Route notes are written by people who have made the journey.

    Plan a step-free journey

The planner tells you what it does not know

Most planners give you one confident answer. Loop gives you three, with the reason each one might be wrong: a lift that has been out since Tuesday, a footbridge closed for works, a bus that has been late four days running.

You can rank routes by time, by number of changes, or by how much walking they involve, and the setting sticks.

Buy the cheapest ticket without doing the arithmetic

Loop watches what you have already paid for today and stops charging once you have reached the daily cap. If a day ticket would have been cheaper than your third single, it tells you before you buy it, not afterwards.

Refunds for cancelled services are applied automatically within a day.
