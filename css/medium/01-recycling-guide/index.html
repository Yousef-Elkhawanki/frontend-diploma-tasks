Waste and recycling

Four bins, one page. Find the thing in your hand and put it in the right one.

Last updated 14 August 2026

Which bin does it go in?

Riverbend collects four streams from every household. Anything in the wrong bin can send the whole load to landfill, so when you are unsure, the grey bin is the safe answer — not the green one.

Green bin — dry recycling

Collected every Tuesday

  • Paper, card and cardboard, flattened
  • Food and drink tins, rinsed
  • Plastic bottles with the lids on
  • Glass jars and bottles, no lids
  • Aerosols, empty

Not this bin: greasy pizza boxes, crisp packets, drinking glasses, mirrors.

Amber bin — garden waste

Collected every other Thursday, March to November

  • Grass cuttings and hedge trimmings
  • Leaves, weeds and dead plants
  • Small branches, under 10 cm across
  • Untreated wood shavings

Not this bin: soil, turf, plant pots, treated or painted wood.

Blue bin — food waste

Collected every Tuesday, all year

  • Everything you did not eat, cooked or raw
  • Bones, shells and peelings
  • Tea leaves and coffee grounds
  • Out-of-date food, out of its packaging

Not this bin: packaging of any kind, liquids, garden waste.

Grey bin — everything else

Collected every other Tuesday

  • Crisp packets, sweet wrappers and film
  • Nappies and wipes
  • Broken crockery and drinking glasses
  • Anything you are genuinely unsure about

Not this bin: batteries, paint, electricals, anything with a plug or a cell.

What happens to it afterwards

  1. Everything is weighed at the Riverbend depot on the morning of collection.
  2. Dry recycling is sorted by hand and machine at Fenland Materials, 12 miles away.
  3. Food waste goes to the anaerobic digester at Marsh Farm and comes back as fertiliser.
  4. Garden waste is composted in open windrows and sold back to residents in spring.
  5. The grey bin goes to the energy-from-waste plant at Northgate. Nothing from Riverbend has gone to landfill since 2023.
