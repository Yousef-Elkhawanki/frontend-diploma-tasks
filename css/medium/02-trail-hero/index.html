Pennine National Park

Fell Ridge Trail

Nine miles over the ridge and back along the river. Four hours of walking, one hour of stopping to look at it.

Read the route description

The walk

Fell Ridge is the ridge you can see from the car park at Hollow Beck, and the walk does the obvious thing: up one end, along the top, down the far side, home along the water.

It is a moorland walk, which means the path is clear in good weather and completely invisible in cloud. There is no shelter between the two stiles at either end of the ridge — about two and a half miles — and no water after the beck.

Distance

9.1 miles / 14.6 km, circular

Two shorter loops of 4 and 6 miles are signed from the same car park.

Ascent

445 m, almost all of it in the first two miles

Steep, stepped path from Hollow Beck to the trig point. Nothing technical.

Time on foot

4 to 5 hours at a steady pace

Add an hour in winter: the ridge holds ice long after the valley has thawed.

People turn back at the trig point because they think the hard part is still coming. It is not. The hard part is behind you and the good part is the next two miles.

Marta Ellis, ranger at Fell Ridge for nineteen years

Stage one — Hollow Beck to the trig point

Cross the footbridge behind the car park and turn immediately uphill. The path is stepped for the first half mile, then becomes a gravel track that zigzags to the shoulder. At the second gate the track ends and the moor begins.

From the gate the trig point is a mile away and visible in anything but cloud. If you cannot see it, this is the point to turn round — the ridge is no place to practise navigation.

Stage two — the ridge and the river

The ridge runs north-east for two and a half miles, level enough that you can look up from your feet. Halfway along, a line of grouse butts marks the boundary of the estate; keep them on your right.

The descent to Wetherfold is steep and loose in two places. At the bottom, join the riverside path and follow it back to Hollow Beck, past the ruined mill and the ford. The last mile is flat and shaded.

Before you go

The weather on the ridge is not the weather in the car park. In the last five years the wind at the trig point has averaged 18 mph higher than at Hollow Beck.

Take with you

  • Boots with an edge — the descent is loose gravel over rock
  • Waterproofs, whatever the forecast says in the valley
  • Two litres of water per person; there is none on the route
  • A paper map and the ability to use it

Parking

Hollow Beck car park, 40 spaces, £4 all day, coins and card. It fills by 09:00 on summer weekends. The overflow field opposite opens at busy times.

Pennine National Park Authority, Hollow Beck Visitor Centre.
