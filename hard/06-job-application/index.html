About the role

Northwind builds websites for museums, universities and three city councils. The six of us on the platform team maintain the component library that every one of those projects is built from, which means the markup you write today is on forty sites by the end of the year.

Roughly half your week is writing HTML, CSS and TypeScript. The other half is the part adverts usually leave out: reading other people's pull requests, testing with a screen reader, writing the guidance that goes next to each component, and occasionally telling a designer that the thing they have drawn cannot be operated with a keyboard. If reviewing markup all Thursday afternoon sounds like a waste of your talents, this is the wrong job — it is most of the value we add.

You do not need to be an accessibility specialist when you start. You do need to be somebody who would rather find out than guess, and who can write down what they found out so the next person does not have to repeat it.

What you will do

  • Build and maintain components that meet WCAG 2.2 level AA before any visual polish is added.
  • Review pull requests from six project teams, focusing on markup semantics.
  • Test with a keyboard and with at least one screen reader, every time.
  • Run a monthly clinic for designers and content editors.
  • Keep the studio's HTML authoring guide current and readable.
  • Pair with a junior developer for two hours a week.

What we are looking for

Essential

You will not get an interview without these.

  • Two or more years writing production HTML and CSS.
  • Confident testing a page with the keyboard alone.
  • Experience of at least one screen reader, on any platform.
  • Able to explain a markup decision in writing, to somebody who is not a developer.
  • Comfortable working in a shared codebase with code review.

Desirable

Nice to have. We will teach you any of these.

  • Experience with design tokens or a shared component library.
  • TypeScript.
  • Public speaking, teaching or technical writing.
  • Worked in or with the public sector.
  • Welsh or British Sign Language.

How we hire

Four stages, about three weeks end to end. You will always know who you are meeting and what they will ask.

  1. Application review. One week. Two people read every application, without your name attached.
  2. Portfolio conversation. 45 minutes, remote, with Yasmin. Talk us through something you built and something you would now build differently.
  3. Take-home markup review. Three hours, paid at £150. We send you a real page from our archive and ask what is wrong with it.
  4. Team interview. 90 minutes, in the studio, with three of the team. Travel costs reimbursed. No whiteboard algorithms.

We tell every applicant the outcome, with feedback, whether or not they reach the interview.

Questions applicants ask

Do I need a degree?

No. Three of the six of us do not have one. We score the take-home task, not your education.

Can I do this job four days a week?

Yes. Two people on the team work a compressed four-day week and one works 0.8 with the salary pro-rated. Say so in your application and it will not count against you.

Do you sponsor visas?

Yes, we hold a skilled worker licence and have sponsored two engineers since 2023. There is a question about this in the form so we can start the paperwork early.

Is the take-home task really paid?

Yes, £150 by bank transfer within a week of you sending it back, whether or not we move forward. If you would rather do a live session than a take-home, ask — we offer both.

What if I need an adjustment during the process?

Tell us in the form, or email Yasmin before you apply. Extra time, questions in advance, a written stage instead of a spoken one, a different room — all of these are normal requests and none of them are recorded against your application.
